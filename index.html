STOP 01 • 1450 BCE Ancient Egypt

Chair of Reniseneb

🪑 OBJECT: Chair of Reniseneb
OBJECTS CAN SHOW POWER
CLUE: Elevated seating = elevated status.

The chair wasn't simply something to sit on. Its height, lion-paw legs and materials communicated authority and status.

Visual: Cora examines the chair with a magnifying glass • POWER ↑
EGYPT • STATUS

Klismos Chair

🪑 OBJECT: Klismos Chair
DESIGN CAN BALANCE BEAUTY + COMFORT
CLUE: Curved legs + sloping backrest.

The form reflects a different relationship between furniture, comfort and civic/social life.

Visual: Cora sits on the chair • FORM ↔ COMFORT
GREECE • COMFORT
STOP 03 • Middle Ages Medieval Europe

Coronation Chair

👑 OBJECT: Coronation Chair
OBJECTS CAN REPRESENT AUTHORITY
CLUE: Monumental form + political symbolism.

STOP 04 • 16th Century Renaissance

Caquetoire

👗 OBJECT: Caquetoire
SOCIETY CHANGES DESIGN
CLUE: Wide seat designed around contemporary clothing.

Furniture responds to fashion, clothing and social interaction. Its flaring seat accommodated voluminous farthingales and skirts.

Visual: Cora measures the chair • BODY → CLOTHING → FURNITURE
RENAISSANCE • SOCIAL LIFE
STOP 05 • Ming Dynasty Ming China

Quanyi Chair

🪵 OBJECT: Quanyi Chair
CRAFT CAN HIDE ITS OWN COMPLEXITY
CLUE: Huanghuali wood + concealed joinery.

Technical mastery can be expressed through restraint rather than decoration. Pressure mortise-and-tenon joints hold it together without nails.

SIMPLE OUTSIDE • COMPLEX INSIDE
MING CHINA • CRAFT
STOP 06 • c. 1859 (TURNING POINT) Industrial Revolution

Thonet No. 14

🏭 OBJECT: Thonet No. 14
MACHINES CHANGE WHO CAN OWN DESIGN
CLUE: Steam-bent wood + standardized components.

Furniture can now be produced consistently and distributed more widely. This is the turning point: the handmade chair becomes standardized, repeatable, and accessible.

Turning Point: STANDARDIZED → REPEATABLE → ACCESSIBLE
THONET • ACCESSIBILITY

STOP 07 • 19th Century Arts & Crafts

The Hand Fights Back

✋ IDEA: The Hand Fights Back
WHO SHOULD MAKE OUR OBJECTS?
CLUE: Industrialization creates a desire to preserve visible human labour, craftsmanship and truth to materials.

John Ruskin and William Morris championed the integrity of manual work in direct opposition to factory alienation.

Visual: 🏭 MACHINE vs. ✋ HUMAN HAND • WHO SHOULD MAKE?
ARTS & CRAFTS • HUMAN HAND
STOP 08 • c. 1890–1910 Art Nouveau

Nature Enters the Machine Age

🌿 IDEA: Nature Enters the Machine Age
ORGANIC LIVING FORMS IN INDUSTRIAL STEEL
CLUE: Flowing curves, organic forms and movement become visual responses to industrial rigidity.

Molten iron poured into whiplash curves, tendrils, and floral silhouettes transforms cold factory architecture into living vines.

Straight factory pipes slowly transform into vines/curves.
ART NOUVEAU • ORGANIC

Beauty, Function & Industry Together

📐 IDEA: What if Beauty + Function + Industry Work Together?
SYNTHESIS AT THE CENTER
CLUE: Bauhaus brings art, craftsmanship and industrial production into one unified design philosophy.

Form follows function. Steel tubes, glass, and geometric proportions replace surface ornamentation with honest production.

The Handmade Watch

⌚ Small Workshops & Skilled Craftspeople
PRECISION WAS PERSONAL
CLUE: Components made individually. Movements assembled and adjusted by hand.

Watches were expensive and varied because skilled labour was central to their production. Each watch was unique.

Movements assembled by master watchmakers • Precision was personal

STOP 11 • 1850 American System of Manufacturing

Waltham Watch Co.

⚙️ Interchangeable Components
TIME BECOMES REPEATABLE
CLUE: The American System introduces specialized machinery, precision gauges and interchangeable components.

By shifting precision from human fingers into dedicated machine tools, timepieces dropped dramatically in cost and spread everywhere.

The American System of Manufacturing: Time becomes repeatable
WALTHAM • PRECISION
